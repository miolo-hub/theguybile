---
label: Chapter 24
title: The Laws of Addiction and Recovery
---

24:1 — Addiction is not a lack of willpower. It is a pattern the brain has learned, and it can be unlearned with help, time and kindness.

24:2 — If thou thinkest, "maybe I drink too much," thou art probably asking the question because something has changed. Listen to it.

24:3 — Warning signs: hiding it, lying about it, needing more, being unable to stop, neglecting work or family, feeling sick or anxious without it. If two or more are true, speak to a doctor.

24:4 — The substances differ: alcohol, nicotine, cannabis, pills, cocaine, gambling, pornography, phones. The pattern is the same. So is the way out: honesty and support.

24:5 — Thou needest not "hit rock bottom" before getting help. Thou mayest step off the elevator at any floor.

24:6 — Doctors, counsellors, support groups, and online communities exist for this. Some drugs and alcohol withdrawals are medically dangerous, so do not quit heavy use cold turkey without medical advice.

24:7 — When a friend is slipping, do not lecture. Say, "I'm worried about you, and I'm here." Offer to go with him to the first appointment. Keep showing up.

24:8 — Never enable. Do not cover for him, lend him money for the habit, or pretend all is well. Love sometimes sounds like "I can't help you hide this."

24:9 — Relapse is not the end, it is a chapter. Dust off, tell somebody, and begin again. Most people who recover tried more than once.

24:10 — Gambling is the cruellest friend: it smiles when thou winnest and steals when thou losest. If thou chasest losses, stop, tell somebody, and block the apps. Free helplines exist in most countries.

24:11 — If thy family is suffering because of thy habit, remember that thou art not a monster. Thou art a man with a problem. Fix it, and thy family will give thee a second chance more often than thou thinkest.

24:12 — Celebrate every sober day, every clean week, every dollar not bet. Small victories add up to a new life.
