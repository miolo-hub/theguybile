---
label: Chapter 25
title: The Laws of Health Scares and Ageing
---

25:1 — When the doctor says a frightening word, thou art allowed to be frightened. Then ask: "What are my options? What happens next? Who can I bring with me?"

25:2 — Bring a friend or a family member to big appointments. Two sets of ears hear twice as much, and one of them will write things down.

25:3 — Get a second opinion on anything serious. A good doctor welcomes it.

25:4 — Do not search symptoms at 2 a.m. and decide thou art dying. Search, then call the doctor in the morning. If the symptom is chest pain, trouble breathing, sudden weakness or confusion, skip the search and call emergency services now.

25:5 — After forty, ask thy doctor which screenings suit thee: blood pressure, cholesterol, blood sugar, colon, prostate, skin. The earlier, the cheaper and kinder the treatment.

25:6 — Hair thins, joints creak, and recovery from a weekend slows. This is not failure, it is mileage. Service the vehicle.

25:7 — A man who is ill needs his brothers more, not less. If thou art sick, tell people. If thy friend is sick, do not say "let me know if you need anything." Say, "I'm bringing dinner Thursday."

25:8 — Make thy will, name thy healthcare proxy, and tell thy family where the important papers are. This is a gift thou givest them in advance.

25:9 — If thy parents grow frail, be patient. They once waited for thee to tie thy shoes. Plan with thy siblings, ask what they want, and share the load.

25:10 — Growing old is a privilege denied to many. Wear the grey hair as a medal.

25:11 — Keep learning to the end. A mind in use stays sharper. Learn a language, a skill, a game, a name.

25:12 — Make peace with thy own mortality while thou art well. It makes the good days sweeter and the bad days smaller.
