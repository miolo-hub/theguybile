---
label: Chapter 27
title: The Laws of Mistakes and Making Amends
---

27:1 — Every man has done something he is ashamed of. The question is not "who is clean," but "who has repaired what he broke."

27:2 — Shame says, "I am bad." Guilt says, "I did something bad." Guilt can be fixed. Shame can only be hidden. Choose guilt, then fix.

27:3 — When thou hast hurt someone, the sequence is: stop the harm, own it, apologise without excuses, ask what would help, and change thy behaviour.

27:4 — An apology is not a purchase. Thou art not owed forgiveness. Give the apology, give time, and accept that some doors stay shut.

27:5 — If thou hast cheated, lied, stolen or betrayed, tell the truth sooner, not later. The cover-up is always worse than the deed.

27:6 — If thou hast hurt someone badly, a lawyer, a counsellor or a mediator may be part of the repair. Do not run from consequences. Taking them is part of becoming whole.

27:7 — Make amends in actions. Show up on time for the next hundred days. Words are the invitation. Habits are the proof.

27:8 — Forgive thyself in proportion. Thou art responsible for thy actions, not for becoming a martyr. Learn, repair, and live usefully.

27:9 — When a friend has wronged thee, tell him plainly. Give him the chance to make it right. Many friendships are deepened by a hard conversation done well.

27:10 — The man who can say "I was wrong, and I'm sorry" is the man everyone wants on their side.

27:11 — Redemption is real. Many of the best men were once worse than thee. They became good by repeating small acts of decency until the habit stuck.
