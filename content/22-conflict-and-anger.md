---
label: Chapter 22
title: The Laws of Conflict and Anger
---

22:1 — Anger is a natural alarm, not a command. It says "something matters here." It does not say "punch something."

22:2 — Before replying in anger, take ten breaths, drink water, or walk around the block. The man who waits ten minutes saves himself ten years of apologies.

22:3 — Say "I feel" instead of "you always." "I feel ignored" starts a talk. "You always ignore me" starts a war.

22:4 — Fight the problem, not the person. The two of you against the dishes, not one of you against the other.

22:5 — Never bring up the past as a weapon. If it was forgiven, it is forgiven. If it was not, say so honestly in a calm place, not in the middle of a row.

22:6 — Raise a problem when it is small. A grievance kept for a year turns into a grenade.

22:7 — Winning an argument and losing the relationship is the worst trade a man can make.

22:8 — A real apology has three parts: what I did, how it hurt thee, and what I will change. "Sorry if you were offended" is not one.

22:9 — If thou canst not stop being angry, or thy temper frightens thy family, get help. Anger management is a skill, not a diagnosis, and counsellors teach it every day.

22:10 — Never use thy size, thy voice, or thy silence as a weapon. Violence, threats and intimidation are never acceptable, in any argument, with any person. This law has no exceptions.

22:11 — If somebody else is the aggressor, walk away when thou canst. The man who leaves a pointless fight is stronger than the man who stays for it.

22:12 — Forgive. Not because they deserve it, but because carrying hate is like carrying hot coal in thy hand, hoping thy enemy gets burned.
