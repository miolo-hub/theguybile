---
label: Chapter 20
title: The Laws of Marriage and Long-Term Love
---

20:1 — Marry thy friend. Passion rises and falls like the tide. A friend is still there at the beach in winter.

20:2 — Before the wedding, discuss money, kids, work, religion, where to live, and who does the dishes. The unromantic questions decide the romantic years.

20:3 — Marriage is not fifty-fifty. It is a hundred-hundred, on different days, in different ways. Some days thou carriest more. Some days she does.

20:4 — Say thank you for the small things: dinner, laundry, the dentist appointment. Gratitude is cheap, and the lack of it is expensive.

20:5 — The Three Words that save marriages are not "I love you." They are "you were right," "I'm sorry," and "what do you need?"

20:6 — Never go to bed angry if thou canst help it. If thou canst not, say "I'm still upset, but I love you, and we'll talk tomorrow."

20:7 — Date thy wife. Weekly if possible, monthly at least. A couple that stops dating becomes roommates with a shared mortgage.

20:8 — Share the invisible load: the planning, the remembering, the booking, the knowing which child needs which shoes. If she is the family's secretary, thou art the intern. Ask for the to-do list, then do things off it unprompted.

20:9 — Intimacy is built outside the bedroom: a hug when thou arrivest, a kiss goodbye, a message at noon. Touch and tenderness are the soil for everything else.

20:10 — Talk about sex honestly. What thou wantest, what thou fearest, what has changed. Partners cannot read minds, and shame makes terrible guesses.

20:11 — Trust is a glass vase. It takes years to make and one second to drop. Be faithful in action, in word, and in thy phone. If thou art tempted, tell a friend, step back, and end the thing before it begins.

20:12 — If the marriage hits a rough patch, go to counselling early, not at the last minute. It is maintenance, not an obituary.

20:13 — Some marriages should end. If there is abuse, repeated betrayal, or no hope left, leaving is not failure. It is honesty. Do it with dignity, and put the children's wellbeing first.

20:14 — In old age, the couple who still hold hands in the supermarket are the real winners. Everything else is noise.
