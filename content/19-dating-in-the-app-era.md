---
label: Chapter 19
title: The Laws of Dating in the App Era
---

19:1 — Thy profile is thy shop window. Use recent photos, one with a smile, one doing something thou lovest, and none with a fish unless thou caught it honestly.

19:2 — Write a bio that sounds like a person, not a LinkedIn summary or a list of demands. Say what thou enjoyest, not what thou despisest.

19:3 — "Hey" is not an opening line. Read her profile and ask about something in it. Effort is the most attractive filter in the world.

19:4 — Never send explicit photos or messages to someone who hasn't clearly asked for them. Doing so is not confidence, it is harassment, and in many places it is illegal.

19:5 — Be who thou art on the first date. The lie thou tellest about thy height, job or age will be discovered on the second.

19:6 — Meet in a public place, tell a friend where thou art, and respect the same caution from her. Safety is not an insult. It is smart.

19:7 — On a first date: ask questions, listen, put the phone away, be kind to the waiter, split the bill or offer to, and do not talk only about thyself or thy ex.

19:8 — If thou art not feeling it, say so kindly. "I enjoyed meeting you, but I don't feel a spark. I wish you well" is five seconds of courage and a lifetime of decency.

19:9 — If she says it is not working for her, say "thanks for telling me." Do not argue, bargain, or send a paragraph. Thou wilt feel a bruise. It will pass.

19:10 — Swiping is a numbers game, but humans are not numbers. Do not treat people like menu items. Each one is somebody's child, and some of them are quite funny.

19:11 — Situationships are two people avoiding one honest conversation. If thou wantest more, say so. If thou wantest less, say that instead. Silence is a decision made cowardly.

19:12 — Take breaks from the apps when they make thee bitter. Thou art not unlovable. The market is noisy, and thou art trying to hear one voice in a stadium.

19:13 — Meet people offline as well: clubs, classes, volunteering, friends of friends. The best relationships often begin at a slow Tuesday event, not at a swipe.
