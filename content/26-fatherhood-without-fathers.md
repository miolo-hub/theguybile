---
label: Chapter 26
title: Fatherhood Without Fathers
---

For the man whose father was absent, dead, cruel, distant or silent. Thou art not alone, and thou art not doomed.

26:1 — Thou didst not choose thy father. Thou canst choose the kind of man thou becomest. This is the greatest freedom thou hast.

26:2 — The absence of a father leaves a hole, and it is natural to feel it. Grieving what thou didst not get is not weakness. It is honesty.

26:3 — Look for good men and learn from them: a coach, an uncle, a teacher, a neighbour, a boss, a friend's father. Many men were fathered by a village.

26:4 — A father may have been hurt before he hurt thee. This explains, but does not excuse. Understanding is for thy peace, not his defence.

26:5 — If thy father is alive and thou wishest to speak with him, thou mayest. Go in with modest hopes and a clear boundary. If he cannot give thee what thou needest, thou mayest grieve that and still keep thy own peace.

26:6 — If thy father was abusive, thy safety and thy healing come first. Thou owest him nothing. Seek a therapist who works with this.

26:7 — When thou becomest a father, thou wilt find thyself saying his words. Pause. Choose better ones.

26:8 — Teach thy children the things nobody taught thee: how to shave, how to talk to a girl, how to cry, how to apologise, how to lose. These small lessons build a whole man.

26:9 — Thou needest not be perfect to break a cycle. Thou needest only be a little better than the last generation, and the generation after thee will be a little better still.

26:10 — If thou art a stepfather, a foster father, or the man who simply showed up, thou art a father in the way that counts. Show up again tomorrow.

26:11 — Some men will read this chapter and think of the father they wished they had. Be that man to someone.
