---
label: Chapter 10
title: The Laws of Fatherhood and Family
---

10:1 — A child does not need a perfect father. A child needs a present one. Show up to the recital, the game and the boring Tuesday.

10:2 — Say "I love you" out loud. Say it to thy kids, thy parents, thy brothers. Men who wait for the perfect moment will wait past the last one.

10:3 — The boy copies what the father does, not what the father says. Thy son is watching how thou treatest his mother, thy waiter, and thyself. Thy daughter is watching what she should expect from men.

10:4 — Apologise to thy children when thou art wrong. It does not break thy authority. It builds their conscience.

10:5 — Change the diaper. Wake for the night feed. Do the school run. These are not "helping." This is fathering.

10:6 — Thy father was a man with flaws, fears and a script he inherited. Forgive what thou canst. Call him if thou canst. Break the cycle for the parts thou canst not.

10:7 — If thy father was absent, harsh or unkind, thou art not doomed to repeat him. Thou art the first man in thy line who gets to choose differently.

10:8 — Call thy mother. Not because she asked. Because she will not always be there to ask.

10:9 — In-laws are the final boss of marriage. Be polite, bring dessert, and never take a side against thy spouse in front of them.

10:10 — Thy ageing parents will one day need thy help. Plan for it with thy siblings before the emergency, not during the hospital visit.

10:11 — Family is not always blood. Some of the best uncles, fathers and brothers in thy life are men who chose thee. Honour them.

10:12 — If thou art not a father, thou mayest still be one to someone: a mentee, a nephew, the kid next door. Boys need many good men, not one.
