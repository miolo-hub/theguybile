---
label: Chapter 11
title: The Laws of Failure, Loss and Grief
---

11:1 — Thou wilt fail. Thou wilt fail publicly and badly. Everyone you admire has a drawer of failures you have never seen.

11:2 — "Failure" is a result, not an identity. Thou didst fail the exam. Thou art not a failure.

11:3 — Fail forward: ask what it taught, fix one thing, try again. Fail sideways: blame the economy, the boss, the ex, the stars. Only one of these moves thee.

11:4 — Grief has no schedule. A man may cry at a funeral, or a month later in a hardware store. Both are the same grief.

11:5 — Thou needest not be "the rock." A rock is silent, cold, and eventually cracks. Be a tree: rooted, bending, and alive.

11:6 — When thy brother loses someone, do not say "everything happens for a reason." Say "I'm here," and then actually be there: bring food, mow the lawn, sit in silence. Show up on day 30, when everyone else has gone home.

11:7 — Divorce, layoffs, diagnoses, and breakups hit like small deaths. Give them the funeral they deserve, then build.

11:8 — Rock bottom is not the end of the story. It is a solid floor from which to push. Many a man built his best life from the worst year of it.

11:9 — Regret is a teacher with a terrible bedside manner. Listen to the lesson, ignore the lecture, and do not let it live in thy house.

11:10 — Death will come for all of us. This is not morbid, it is motivating. Call the friend. Write the letter. Take the trip. Go to the party. The list does not get shorter by waiting.
