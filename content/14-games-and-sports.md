---
label: Chapter 14
title: The Laws of Games and Sports
---

14:1 — It is only a game. Say it three times before thou throwest the controller.

14:2 — Whoever wins shall not gloat for more than ninety seconds. Whoever loses shall not blame the lag for more than ninety seconds either.

14:3 — A man may support a team that has not won in thirty years. This is called faith, and it is the purest form of it known to science.

14:4 — Do not rub a rival fan's nose in a loss unless the loss is truly historic. If it is historic, wait one day, then do it gently.

14:5 — In fantasy leagues, the man who trades away thy best player in a "fair swap" is not a friend. He is a fantasy general manager. Treat accordingly.

14:6 — Play with people better than thee. Thy skill rises, thy ego shrinks, and both are good for thee.

14:7 — The friend who has played one game for one week and now explains it to thee shall be nodded at and ignored.

14:8 — In pick-up games, thou shalt call thy own fouls. The man who cheats a casual match cheats at everything.

14:9 — Gaming hours are fine. Gaming at the cost of thy job, thy sleep and thy human relationships is a quiet fire. Check the clock before thou checkest the "one more match."

14:10 — Sport teaches the lessons nothing else can: losing with grace, winning with humility, and showing up on cold mornings. Teach thy kids that, not just the score.

14:11 — The oldest man at the park, still playing at sixty, is the one to follow. Stay in the game as long as thy body allows.
