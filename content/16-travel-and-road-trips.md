---
label: Chapter 16
title: The Laws of Travel and Road Trips
---

16:1 — Shotgun is called outside, in sight of the car, and expires after ten minutes. The driver, however, may veto anything.

16:2 — The driver controls the music. The co-pilot controls the navigation. The back seat controls nothing but the snacks, and the snacks are the true power.

16:3 — Never trust a man who says "I know a shortcut." He has never known a shortcut.

16:4 — Split costs fairly and in writing. The man who says "we'll sort it out later" shall be hunted down in March.

16:5 — Pack light. Thou wilt wear three shirts and carry twelve. The man with a huge suitcase is the man who will carry it up four flights of stairs, alone.

16:6 — Arrive early at the airport. The man who says "we have plenty of time" is about to sprint.

16:7 — When in a foreign land, learn five words of the language: hello, please, thank you, sorry, and beer. Locals will forgive many mistakes if thou art polite.

16:8 — Respect the place. Do not be the loud tourist, the litterer, or the man who climbs where signs say not to. Leave the beach cleaner than thou foundest it.

16:9 — Travel with thy brothers at least once a year. Memories made on the road outlast any gadget. The worst day of the trip is the best story at the next wedding.

16:10 — Travel alone at least once in thy life. Eat at a counter, get lost, talk to a stranger. Thou wilt learn who thou art when no one is watching.

16:11 — Lose thy wallet, thy phone, or thy flight, and thou wilt learn that most problems are fixed by calm, a smile and one very patient person at a desk.
