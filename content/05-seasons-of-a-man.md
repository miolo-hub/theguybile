---
label: Chapter 5
title: The Seasons of a Man
---

A man is not one man. He is five or six men in a trench coat, each of whom thinks he has it figured out.

### The Boy (up to 12)

5:1 — Play. Fall. Get dirty. A scraped knee is a free lesson in consequences.

5:2 — Cry when it hurts. The boy who is told to stop crying becomes the man who cannot start.

### The Teenager (13–19)

5:3 — Thou art not the main character. Thou art a supporting actor in forty other people's stories, and it is okay.

5:4 — Acne passes. Bad decisions linger. Think twice before posting, texting, or tattooing.

5:5 — The loudest boy in the room is usually the most scared. Be kind to him anyway.

### The Twenties

5:6 — Nobody your age knows what they are doing. The ones who look certain are better at hiding it.

5:7 — Start saving now, even if it is small. Compound interest is the only magic that is actually real.

5:8 — Say yes to experiences, no to debts, and maybe to the third drink.

5:9 — Take the job that teaches you, not only the one that pays you. Pay catches up. Skills compound.

### The Thirties

5:10 — Thy friends will begin to disappear into weddings, babies and mortgages. Schedule them anyway. Friendship in your thirties is a calendar invite or it is nothing.

5:11 — Thy back will make a noise one morning for no reason. This is the First Sign. Stretch.

5:12 — Thou shalt stop being impressed by thy own busyness. Busy is not important. Busy is a state of calendar.

### The Forties

5:13 — The crisis will arrive at about 43, wearing a leather jacket and holding a motorcycle brochure. Talk to a friend, a doctor or a therapist first. Buy the bike second, if still needed.

5:14 — Get thy blood work done. Cholesterol does not care how you feel.

5:15 — Rediscover a thing you loved at 16. Guitar, soccer, fishing, bad movies. Joy is not wasted on the middle-aged.

### The Fifties and Beyond

5:16 — Retirement is not the end of purpose. A man who stops being needed starts to fade, so find something to be needed for.

5:17 — Teach someone younger. The things you learned the hard way are worth more to him than a library.

5:18 — The friends who are still here at 60 are the ones you called at 40. Keep calling.

5:19 — At every age: be kind, be curious, and be willing to be a beginner again.
