Short stories for the road, the campfire and the third round.

### The Parable of the Last Slice

There were four brothers and a pizza with one slice left. All four said, "No, you take it." Twenty minutes passed. The pizza went cold, and then it went to the roommate who walked in and said nothing. And the brothers learned: **Guybile 3:2** exists for a reason. Claim the slice, or lose it to a stranger.

### The Parable of Five Minutes Away

A man texted, "5 min away." His friends set a timer. Forty-five minutes later he arrived, glowing, carrying a bag of ice nobody had asked for. "Traffic," he said. There was no traffic. He had been looking for his keys. And the friends learned: never ask a man where he is. Ask where his keys are.

### The Parable of the Ticket Man

Dave said, "I'll handle the tickets." The group went to bed content. On the day of the concert, Dave looked at his phone and said, "Oh." The group sat in a car park listening to the concert through a fence. And from then on, every group needed one man who handles the tickets, and a second man who checks that Dave did.

### The Parable of I'm Fine

A man said "I'm fine" for a month. His friends saw his eyes and said nothing. One friend, called Sam, drove over with a bag of crisps and said, "You don't look fine." The man sat on the sofa, and for forty minutes said everything he had been carrying. Sam said very little. At the end the man said, "Thanks." Sam said, "Anytime." And the friendship doubled that night.

### The Parable of the Tool Lent and Never Returned

A man lent his neighbour a drill. A year later he saw it in the neighbour's garage and said nothing. Another year passed. At the neighbour's funeral, the widow gave him the drill, with a note: "He said you were the best friend he ever had." And the man wept, and kept the drill, and never lent another one without a smile.

### The Parable of the Group Chat That Went Silent

A group chat had been dead for six months. One man typed, "Anyone alive?" Nothing. Then, a thumbs-up. Then, "Honestly, I was about to say the same." Within a week, they had a dinner booked. And the brothers learned that every silent friend is waiting for someone to speak first.

### The Parable of the Man Who Asked for Help

A proud man was moving house alone. He carried the sofa halfway up the stairs and got stuck. For an hour he refused to call anyone. Finally he called one friend, who arrived in ten minutes with two more. The sofa was up in five. Nobody said a word about it. And the man learned that asking for help does not spend thy dignity. It buys thee friends.
