---
label: Chapter 18
title: The Laws of Social Media and the Internet
---

18:1 — Think before thou postest. The internet is a tattoo parlour with no memory of regret.

18:2 — Never argue with a stranger in the comments. Thou wilt not change his mind. Thou wilt only lose an hour and thy blood pressure.

18:3 — If a man online promises thee riches in thirty days, a six-pack in ten, or a girlfriend with one weird trick, thou art the product, not the customer.

18:4 — The algorithm is designed to keep thee angry, envious and scrolling. Know thy enemy. Set a daily limit and honour it.

18:5 — Verify before thou sharest. A man who forwards false news is a messenger for the liar. Pause, check, then share.

18:6 — Never post a photo, a rumour or a message about a person that thou wouldst not say to their face. Never share anyone's private images. Doing so is cruel, and in many places it is a crime.

18:7 — Do not send what thou wouldst not want read aloud in court or at thy mother's table. Messages last longer than moods.

18:8 — Online, the angry mob is made of tired people with the same fears as thee. Do not join the pile-on. Be the man who says, "let's not."

18:9 — Delete the app for a week. Observe thy mood. Observe thy sleep. Observe how many things happen without thee knowing and without consequence.

18:10 — A man with a thousand followers and no friends is poorer than a man with three friends and no followers.

18:11 — If a younger boy looks to thee online, be what thou wishest the internet had shown thee at fifteen: kind, honest, and not selling anything.
