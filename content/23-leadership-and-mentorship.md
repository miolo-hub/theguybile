---
label: Chapter 23
title: The Laws of Leadership and Mentorship
---

23:1 — A leader eats last. If thy team is hungry, tired or scared, thy job is to notice before they say so.

23:2 — Take the blame, share the credit. This sentence has made more careers than any degree.

23:3 — Praise in public, criticise in private. Do both often and sincerely.

23:4 — Say "I don't know, let's find out." Teams trust a leader who admits gaps more than one who bluffs.

23:5 — Listen first. Most good ideas live in the quietest person in the room, who is waiting to be asked.

23:6 — Treat everyone, from the intern to the CEO, as if they had the power to hire or fire thee. Someday they might.

23:7 — The boss who emails at midnight teaches his team to answer at midnight. Model the rest thou wishest them to take.

23:8 — Hire for kindness and curiosity. Skills can be taught. Character cannot, or only at enormous expense.

23:9 — Be a mentor. Find one person younger than thee and offer ten minutes a month. Thou wilt change a life, and it will cost thee a coffee.

23:10 — Find a mentor. Ask a person thou admirest, and make it easy: one specific question, a short call, a thank-you note.

23:11 — When a woman is interrupted in a meeting, say, "I'd like to hear the rest of her point." This one sentence is a gift to the room.

23:12 — Leadership is not the corner office. It is the man in the room who stays calm when everyone else is not.
