---
label: Chapter 8
title: The Laws of the Mind
---

Men die by suicide at a much higher rate than women in most countries, and they are less likely to see a doctor or talk to anyone first. The tough-guy script is not a flex. It is a hazard. This chapter is the most important one in the book.

8:1 — Feelings are data, not weakness. Anger is often sadness wearing a hard hat. Ask what is underneath it.

8:2 — Thou shalt cry. Alone, with a brother, at a movie about a dog. Crying releases tension, and every man who has done it will tell you that nothing fell apart.

8:3 — "Man up" is the worst advice ever given. It has never helped one man. It has hurt millions. Replace it with "talk to me."

8:4 — Therapy is not for the broken. It is for the human. A man who services his car every year can service his mind once in a while.

8:5 — If thou hast been sad, empty or numb for two weeks or more, that is not a mood. That is a signal. Speak to a doctor, a therapist or a person thou trust.

8:6 — If thou art ever thinking of ending thy life, or of harming thyself, tell someone right now. A friend, a family member, a doctor, or a crisis line (in the United States, call or text 988; elsewhere, search "crisis line" plus thy country). Thy pain is real and so is the help. Thou art needed here.

8:7 — Alcohol is a lousy therapist. It tells you everything is fine, then charges you at 4 a.m. If thou drinkest to feel normal, or cannot stop at one, tell somebody.

8:8 — Porn, gambling, gaming and doomscrolling are designed by teams of clever people to take thy hours. Thou art allowed to say "enough," and to ask for help saying it.

8:9 — Research links long-term loneliness to worse health, and some studies compare it to smoking. Thou art not weak for being lonely. Thou art human. Join a club, a gym, a church, a league, a volunteer crew.

8:10 — Thy thoughts are not all true. At 3 a.m., the brain is a liar with a microphone. Wait until daylight before believing anything it says.

8:11 — Comparing thy insides to other men's outsides is a losing game. They have a panic attack in the car too. They just do not post it.

8:12 — The strongest sentence in the English language is "I need help." Practise saying it on small things first: a stuck jar, a moving day, a sad Tuesday.

8:13 — Touch grass. Literally. Walk outside for twenty minutes. This is clinically underrated.

8:14 — Forgive thyself. The man thou wert at nineteen did the best he could with the tools he had. Treat him like a younger brother, not an enemy.
