---
label: Chapter 17
title: The Laws of Weddings, Bachelor Parties and Funerals
---

### Weddings

17:1 — The best man's duties are: the ring, the speech, the groom's sanity, and the morning-after water. In that order, with the ring first.

17:2 — The speech shall be under five minutes. It shall contain one joke, one true story, and one sincere sentence. It shall not contain the ex, the arrest, or the phrase "and then it all went wrong."

17:3 — Dress properly. Iron the shirt. A well-fitted suit has made more men look confident than any pep talk.

17:4 — Dance. Badly, joyfully, without a care. Nobody remembers the best dancer. Everybody remembers the man who refused.

17:5 — Do not upstage the couple. It is their day. Thy job is to make it easier, not more about thee.

### Bachelor Parties

17:6 — A good bachelor party leaves the groom alive, faithful, solvent, and on time for the wedding. All else is bonus.

17:7 — Plan it around what the groom loves, not what the loudest bro loves. He is the guest of honour, not the entertainment.

17:8 — Budget it so the cheapest friend can come. The man who cannot afford it will not say so. Ask quietly.

### Funerals

17:9 — Go. Thou wilt not know what to say. That is fine. Thy presence is the message.

17:10 — Say simple words: "I'm sorry. He mattered to me. I'm here." Skip the "he's in a better place" unless thou knowest the family wants it.

17:11 — Funerals are where men cry in public without shame. Join them. Hold a friend's shoulder. Share a story. Laugh at the good ones. This is how the dead stay alive.

17:12 — Tell the people thou lovest that thou lovest them, before the next funeral is thine.
