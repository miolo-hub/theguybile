---
label: Chapter 21
title: The Laws of Friendship After Thirty
---

21:1 — Making friends as an adult is awkward by design. Nobody hands thee a locker and a lunch table. Thou must be the one who says, "want to grab a coffee?"

21:2 — Friendship is built on repeated, unplanned time. Join a regular thing: a five-a-side, a run club, a board-game night, a choir, a class. Show up every week, and the rest follows.

21:3 — Be the one who invites. Somebody has to, and the man who waits for an invitation will wait until he is sixty.

21:4 — Don't keep score of who texted last. Be generous. The friend who cannot reply this month may be drowning.

21:5 — The twenty-minute call while walking the dog is a legitimate friendship. Ten of them beat one grand reunion.

21:6 — Say "I miss you" out loud. It takes three seconds, and nine out of ten men have been waiting for someone else to say it first.

21:7 — Not every friendship lasts forever. Some are for a season. Thank them, wish them well, and make room for new ones.

21:8 — Have at least one friend thou canst call about the thing thou art embarrassed about. If thou hast none, make that friend. This is the most important project in thy social life.

21:9 — Befriend men older and younger than thee. The old teach thee what lasts. The young teach thee what is coming.

21:10 — Remember birthdays, kids' names, and the thing they said they were nervous about. These small details are the whole of friendship.

21:11 — When a friend moves, marries or has a baby, thy friendship changes shape. It does not die. Learn the new shape.
