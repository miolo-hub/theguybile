title: The History of Man
---

A short, loose tour of how men got here, so thou knowest that the rules were invented, and what is invented can be rewritten. Dates and details are broad strokes, not a history exam.

12:1 — **The Hunters.** For most of human history men hunted, gathered, fought and fixed things, and survived by cooperating in small groups. The lone wolf is a modern myth. Early man lived or died by the tribe.

12:2 — **The Farmers.** When we settled down, property, inheritance and "who owns what" appeared, and so did many of the rules about who gets power. Much of what we call "traditional manhood" is a few thousand years of farm logistics.

12:3 — **The Philosophers.** The Stoics, like Marcus Aurelius and Seneca, taught men to master their reactions, not their surroundings. Notice: they wrote their feelings in journals. The original stoic man had a diary.

12:4 — **The Knights.** Medieval codes of chivalry asked men to be fierce in battle and gentle in courtship. Even then, we knew a man needed both edges.

12:5 — **The Factory.** The Industrial Revolution pulled men out of homes and fields into factories. The "breadwinner" was born: a man defined by his paycheck, often absent from his own children. That identity is only about two hundred years old, and it is now wobbling.

12:6 — **The Wars.** The twentieth century sent millions of men to wars and told them to come home and say nothing. The result was silence, drinking and trauma passed quietly down to sons.

12:7 — **The Mirror.** From the 1960s onward, women's movements asked tough questions of every man: who does the housework, who holds the power, who gets heard. Many men felt attacked. The wiser ones realised they were being invited to a bigger life.

12:8 — **The Screen.** Today's man is told to be a provider, a lover, a feminist, a father, a hustler, a monk and a fit person with abs, all at once, while his phone shows him strangers living better. No wonder he is tired. The confusion is real. The way out is not going backwards or giving up. It is choosing, on purpose, which man to be.

### The Psychology, in Brief

12:9 — Psychologists have a name for men who cannot put words to their feelings: normative male alexithymia. In bro terms, "I don't know, I just feel weird." Practise naming the feeling: angry, sad, ashamed, scared, lonely, tired. Naming it shrinks it.

12:10 — Researchers also describe "gender role strain," the stress of trying to meet a rigid picture of manhood nobody can actually meet. If thou feelest like thou art failing at "being a man," the problem may be the picture, not thee.

12:11 — Carl Jung wrote of archetypes. Later writers described the mature man as a blend of four: the King (order and blessing), the Warrior (discipline and courage), the Magician (wisdom and skill), and the Lover (connection and joy). A man missing any one of these limps. A man who overdoes one becomes a tyrant, a bully, a manipulator or a mess.

12:12 — Erik Erikson described life as a series of tasks: trust, independence, identity, intimacy, contribution, and finally integrity. Thou art not behind. Thou art on thy own task. Do the one in front of thee.

12:13 — Attachment theory says how we were loved as children shapes how we love as adults. If thou findest thyself clingy, avoidant or explosive in relationships, it is not a life sentence. Patterns can be learned and unlearned, with time and sometimes with a therapist.

12:14 — The word "toxic" does not mean "male." It means the parts of the old script that hurt men and the people around them: never ask for help, never show weakness, dominate or be dominated. Keep the good (courage, loyalty, responsibility) and drop the rest.
